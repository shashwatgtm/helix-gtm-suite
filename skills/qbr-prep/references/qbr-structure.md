# GUARD QBR/EBR Structure — Review Mode Reference

## 7-Section QBR Template

Every QBR follows this structure. Adapt depth based on account tier and audience.

### Section 1: Partnership Summary (1 slide / 1 paragraph)
- Relationship timeline (contract start, renewal date, months together)
- Key milestones since last QBR
- Stakeholder map update (who is in the room, who should be)
- Set the tone: collaborative review, not vendor report

### Section 2: Value Delivered (2 slides / 2-3 paragraphs)
- Map delivered outcomes to original business case / success criteria
- Use customer's own metrics and language, not vendor metrics
- Quantify: hours saved, revenue impact, cost reduction, efficiency gain
- Include at least one specific win story with before/after data
- If ROI is unclear, acknowledge the gap and propose measurement plan

### Section 3: Product Adoption (1-2 slides / 1-2 paragraphs)
- Feature utilization breakdown: what they use, what they don't
- Usage trends: growing, stable, or declining (with context)
- Identify the "hero feature" — the one driving the most value
- Identify the "untapped feature" — the biggest stickiness or expansion opportunity
- Benchmark against similar customers if data available (anonymized)

### Section 4: Support & Satisfaction (1 slide / 1 paragraph)
- Ticket volume and resolution trends
- CSAT/NPS scores with trajectory
- Open escalations (if any) with status and timeline
- Be transparent about issues — customers know when you are hiding problems

### Section 5: Risk & Mitigation (1 slide / 1-2 paragraphs)
- Proactive risk flags with proposed actions
- Champion/stakeholder changes
- Competitive landscape (if relevant)
- Do NOT skip this section even for healthy accounts. Surface small risks before they grow.

### Section 6: Growth Opportunities (1 slide / 1 paragraph)
- Natural expansion moments based on usage patterns
- New use cases or departments that could benefit
- Upcoming product capabilities that align with their goals
- Frame as value extension, not upsell pitch

### Section 7: Forward Plan (1 slide / 1-2 paragraphs)
- Next quarter objectives (mutual, not one-sided)
- Specific action items with owners and deadlines
- Success metrics for next QBR
- Meeting cadence confirmation

---

## Audience Adaptation

### Standard QBR (Ops lead / Primary contact)
- Full 7-section depth
- Data-heavy, tactical
- 30-45 minutes

### Executive Business Review (C-suite / VP)
- Sections 1, 2, 5, 7 emphasized
- Section 3-4 condensed to key headlines
- Strategic framing, business outcomes language
- 20-30 minutes

### At-Risk Account QBR
- Lead with Section 5 (risks), then Section 2 (value)
- Replace Section 6 with "Recovery Plan"
- Include specific save commitments
- Do NOT pitch expansion during an at-risk QBR

### First QBR (90 days post-onboarding)
- Section 1 emphasizes onboarding completion and baseline establishment
- Section 2 focuses on time-to-value milestones achieved
- Section 7 establishes the ongoing review cadence

---

## Output Formats

Based on user request, produce:
- **QBR brief (text):** Narrative format with all 7 sections as prose
- **QBR talking points:** Bullet-format cheat sheet for the CSM to reference during the meeting
- **QBR slide outline:** Section headers + key points + suggested data visuals per slide
- **Post-QBR recap email:** Summary of discussion, action items, next steps (send within 24h)

## Time Budget Rule

Total QBR prep should take under 30 minutes with this skill. If the CSM is spending more than 30 minutes, the skill is not doing its job. The skill replaces the data-gathering and structuring work — the CSM adds relationship context and judgment.
