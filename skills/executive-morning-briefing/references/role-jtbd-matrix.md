# JTBD Matrix by Role and Level

This reference contains the full Jobs-to-be-Done priority matrix for each role and level.
Referenced from SKILL.md — loaded when the skill needs role-specific prioritization.

## Jobs To Be Done by Role and Level

### Sales

**Sales IC (AE, SDR, BDR)**

Core anxiety: "Did any of my prospects respond? What deals need action today?"

Priority order:
1. Prospect/customer responses to their outreach (email, LinkedIn, calls returned)
2. Their deals with activity overnight (Slack mentions, internal chatter)
3. Their tasks and follow-ups due today
4. Prep for their meetings (discovery calls, demos, negotiations)
5. Quota/pipeline check (their number, not company-wide)
6. Team FYI (wins, competitive intel relevant to their deals)

What they don't need: company MRR, other reps' deals, cross-functional blockers outside their scope.

**Sales Manager**

Core anxiety: "Is my team on track? Who's stuck? Any escalations landing on me?"

Priority order:
1. Rep blocked or deal at risk (they can help unblock)
2. Escalations—customer complaints, executive asks about their team
3. Team metrics vs quota (pacing, forecast risk)
4. Their own deals (if player-coach)
5. Prep for pipeline reviews, 1:1s, team meetings
6. Cross-team items affecting their team (marketing leads quality, product issues)

What they don't need: other managers' teams, board-level metrics, executive politics.

**Sales Executive (CRO, VP Sales, Head of Sales)**

Core anxiety: "Is the number in danger? What's the board going to ask about?"

Priority order:
1. Pipeline and revenue metrics (company-wide, trends, anomalies)
2. Large deal escalations (enterprise accounts, churn risk, competitive losses)
3. Cross-functional blockers (pricing, legal, product gaps)
4. Forecast accuracy (are commits real?)
5. Team health (attrition signals, rep performance patterns)
6. Board/investor items, competitive intelligence

### Marketing

**Marketing IC (Content Writer, Campaign Manager, Specialist)**

Core anxiety: "What's due today? What approvals am I waiting on?"

Priority order:
1. Deadlines due today (content, campaigns, deliverables)
2. Approvals they're waiting on (blocking their work)
3. Feedback on their work (comments, reviews, revisions)
4. Their campaign performance (if running active campaigns)
5. Prep for their meetings
6. Team FYI (brand news, launches relevant to their work)

What they don't need: MQL targets, pipeline contribution, executive metrics.

**Marketing Manager**

Core anxiety: "Are my campaigns working? Is my team blocked? What's the lead?"

Priority order:
1. Team member blocked (approvals needed from them)
2. Campaign performance off track (their campaigns)
3. Deadlines at risk across their team
4. Their own deliverables
5. Cross-team dependencies (content waiting on product, design waiting on brand)
6. Prep for reviews, 1:1s, stakeholder meetings

**Marketing Executive (CMO, VP Marketing)**

Core anxiety: "Are we hitting pipeline contribution? Any brand fires? What's the board story?"

Priority order:
1. Pipeline and MQL metrics (company targets)
2. Brand/PR issues (reputation, media, crises)
3. Campaign performance (material impact only)
4. Budget and spend efficiency
5. Cross-functional blockers (sales alignment, product marketing gaps)
6. Team health, board prep, strategic items

### Demand Gen

**Demand Gen IC (Specialist, Campaign Manager)**

Core anxiety: "Are my campaigns performing? Am I on budget? What needs optimization?"

Priority order:
1. Campaign alerts (spend anomalies, performance drops, budget overruns)
2. Their channels' metrics (CPL, CAC, conversion rates for their campaigns)
3. Tasks and optimizations due today
4. A/B tests ready for decisions
5. Prep for their meetings
6. Channel news (platform changes, algorithm shifts)

What they don't need: company pipeline, other specialists' campaigns, executive metrics.

**Demand Gen Manager**

Core anxiety: "Is the team hitting targets? Which channels are working? Where do we need to shift?"

Priority order:
1. Team metrics vs target (MQL pacing, spend efficiency)
2. Channel performance across the team (what's working, what's not)
3. Team member blocked or campaign at risk
4. Budget pacing (overall, not just one campaign)
5. Their own campaigns (if player-coach)
6. Cross-team (sales feedback on lead quality, marketing alignment)

**Demand Gen Executive (VP Growth, Head of Demand Gen)**

Core anxiety: "Are we efficient at scale? What's the CAC trend? Where should we invest?"

Priority order:
1. Funnel metrics (CAC, LTV, payback, efficiency ratios)
2. Channel mix and attribution (strategic allocation)
3. Budget vs performance (material variances)
4. Pipeline contribution (demand gen's piece of the number)
5. Team capacity and scaling
6. Board/investor metrics, competitive positioning

### Customer Success

**CS IC (CSM)**

Core anxiety: "Did any accounts flag overnight? Any renewals approaching? Any escalations?"

Priority order:
1. At-risk accounts (health score drops, usage declines)
2. Renewal dates approaching (90/60/30 day windows)
3. Customer escalations and support tickets for their book
4. QBR prep (upcoming reviews)
5. Expansion signals (usage growth, new department interest)
6. Onboarding milestones for new customers

What they don't need: company NRR, other CSMs' accounts, executive board narratives.

**CS Manager**

Core anxiety: "Is my team hitting GRR? Which CSMs need help? Any churn risk across the portfolio?"

Priority order:
1. Portfolio-level churn risk (accounts at risk across the team)
2. Renewal forecast (GRR pacing)
3. Escalations landing on them
4. Rep performance (account health trends per CSM)
5. Expansion pipeline
6. Cross-functional items (product bugs affecting customers, sales handoff issues)

What they don't need: other managers' books, board-level NRR narrative, executive politics.

**CS Executive (CCO, VP CS)**

Core anxiety: "What's NRR trending? Any logo churn risk? What's the board story?"

Priority order:
1. NRR/GRR metrics (company-wide, trends)
2. High-value churn risk (enterprise accounts)
3. Expansion pipeline and revenue
4. CS team health (capacity, attrition)
5. Board/investor items
6. Cross-functional (product roadmap gaps, sales alignment)

### General / Cofounder / CEO

Core anxiety: "What's the state of the business? What could blow up? What am I forgetting?"

Priority order:
1. Cash and runway (if relevant)
2. Revenue metrics (MRR, churn, pipeline)
3. Customer escalations (churn risk, PR risk)
4. Cross-functional blockers (they're often the only unlocker)
5. Board/investor items
6. Team health (attrition, major wins, culture signals)
7. External commitments (press, events, key customer meetings)

### Uncategorizable Data

Sometimes data doesn't fit neatly into priority buckets:

**Random Slack mentions:** Someone tagged the user in an off-topic thread, social message, or unclear context.
- If clearly social/off-topic: Omit or put in "Safe to ignore"
- If unclear: Brief mention at end: "Also, Jordan mentioned you in #random—didn't look urgent but I couldn't tell the context."

**Ambiguous urgency:** Item could be urgent or could be nothing—depends on context you don't have.
- Surface it with uncertainty: "Not sure if this matters: [item]. Worth a look?"

**Personal items in work feeds:** Doctor appointment on work calendar, personal errand in task list.
- Default: Omit from briefing
- Exception: If it affects work schedule ("You have a 2pm appointment—calendar shows you're out 1:30-3:30"), mention the block without details.

**Data from unknown sources:** Integration returns data you can't categorize.
- Don't invent categories
- Group at end: "A few other things came through that I couldn't prioritize: [brief list]"

**Rule:** When in doubt, surface briefly rather than hide. Let the user decide if it matters.
