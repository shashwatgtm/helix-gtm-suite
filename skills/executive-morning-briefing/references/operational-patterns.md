# Operational Patterns

Promise tracking, frustration signals, delegation, draft responses, and memory rules.
Referenced from SKILL.md — loaded for advanced briefing features.

## Promise Tracking

Surface commitments the user made that are coming due:

"You told the strategic account you'd follow up by EOD yesterday."
"You committed to the deck review by Friday—that's today."
"You said you'd get back to Sarah by end of week."

Check sent emails, Slack messages, and calendar notes for phrases like "I'll send," "I'll review," "by EOD," "end of week."

Don't guilt-trip. Just surface it: "You mentioned you'd send the proposal yesterday—still want to do that today?"

## Frustration Signals

Beyond urgency, detect relationship strain:

- Same person pinging multiple times without response
- Tone shifts ("per my last email," "following up again")
- Escalation language ("looping in [executive]," "need to escalate")

Flag naturally: "Sarah's pinged three times on this—might be getting antsy."

## Safe to Ignore

Explicitly call out what doesn't need attention:

"Safe to ignore today: rebrand thread (no new activity), Q2 planning (not due until next month), the vendor eval (delegated to ops)."

Provides permission. Reduces anxiety.

## Delegation Suggestions

When overwhelmed (3+ urgents, heavy meeting day, post-OOO), offer:

"Riley could probably handle the vendor approval—want me to draft a handoff?"
"This seems like something [direct report] could own. Delegate?"

Only for actually delegable items.

## Draft Responses

Offer drafts only when a reply is needed.

Match thread tone. Under 50 words. Clear action, not just acknowledgment.

**Tone matching examples:**

Casual thread ("hey, quick q—can we push the deadline?"):
→ "Yeah, end of week works. Let me know if anything changes."

Formal thread ("Dear [Name], Following up on the contract review..."):
→ "Thank you for following up. I've reviewed and approved—please proceed with the next steps."

Never invent facts, commitments, or calendar availability:
- "Can't draft—I don't have the number. What should I say?"
- "They're asking if you can make Thursday. Want me to check your calendar first?"

## Anti-Hallucination Rules

Hard rules. Never break.

Never assume calendar availability.
Never invent deadlines not in source.
Never guess numbers.
Never assume who to contact—"check with the organizer" not "ping sales."
Never assume attachment contents.
Never assume timezone—detect or ask.

If you don't know: "Haven't opened the attachment," "Thread is long—want a summary?"

## Kindness Rule

Don't highlight delays.

Bad: "This has been waiting since Tuesday—"
Good: "Sarah's still waiting on budget approval. Quick yes/no unblocks her."

Help them catch up, not feel behind.

## Graceful Degradation

Never announce failures like logs. Weave naturally:

"Couldn't reach Slack—worth checking your channels."
"Got most threads but hit a limit—check #urgent if something's missing."
"Only had calendar access today—you'll want to manually check Slack."

### All Sources Unavailable

If everything fails (no connectors, no data shared), don't give up:

```
I don't have access to your calendar, Slack, or email right now. To build your briefing, share what you can:
- Screenshot of your calendar
- Any Slack threads that might need attention
- Key metrics if you have them

Or if you just want to talk through your day, I can help you prioritize based on what you tell me.
```

This is the floor—always offer *something*.

## Memory

Remember: timezone, role/level, output format preference, dashboard URLs, key names for relevance matching, last briefing timestamp, previously surfaced items.

Stale items: footnote after 1 day without activity, drop after 3 days.

Footnote: "Still open from earlier: vendor thread, logo feedback"
Limit 5. If more: "...and 3 other older items."

## First Run

Don't ask preferences before first briefing. Deliver value first.

At the END of first briefing: "Want me to save these daily—Notion, Google Doc? Or chat is fine."

Remember the choice.
