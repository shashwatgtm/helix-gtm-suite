# GUARD Expansion Signal Model — Uplift Mode Reference

## 4-Signal Expansion Framework

### Signal 1: Usage-Based Signals (Strongest)
Quantitative indicators from product data:
- Approaching or exceeding plan limits (seats, API calls, storage, credits)
- New feature adoption in advanced/premium modules
- Power users emerging (high-frequency, deep usage)
- Integration expansion (connecting to more systems)
- Usage growing faster than contracted entitlement

**Action timing:** When usage exceeds 70% of entitlement, open the conversation. At 90%, it becomes urgent.

### Signal 2: Organization-Based Signals
Changes in the customer's company that create expansion potential:
- New departments showing interest in the product
- Company headcount growing (hiring signals from LinkedIn, job postings)
- M&A activity (acquirer brings new users)
- New strategic initiatives announced (digital transformation, cost optimization)
- Leadership changes that bring fresh budget or mandate

**Action timing:** When organizational change is confirmed, not rumored. Reach out within 2 weeks of signal.

### Signal 3: Value-Based Signals
Evidence that the customer has achieved enough value to justify more:
- Documented ROI exceeding original business case
- Customer willing to be a reference or case study
- Unsolicited positive feedback to their leadership
- Presenting your solution at industry events
- Referring peers or other companies

**Action timing:** After a major value milestone is documented. Strike while enthusiasm is high.

### Signal 4: Timing-Based Signals
Calendar and contract events that create natural expansion moments:
- Budget planning cycle (fiscal year planning, Q1 budget allocation)
- QBR where growth opportunities are discussed
- Mid-contract when new needs emerge
- Renewal conversation (bundle expansion with renewal for better terms)
- Strategic planning offsite (customer's annual planning)

**Action timing:** 4-6 weeks before the budget/planning event.

## Expansion Readiness Score

| Score | Classification | Signals Present | Recommended Approach |
|-------|---------------|-----------------|---------------------|
| 4/4 signals | Hot | All four signal types firing | Immediate expansion conversation with prepared business case |
| 3/4 signals | Warm | Three of four present | Proactive outreach, build business case, identify decision-maker |
| 2/4 signals | Developing | Two present | Nurture — reinforce value, seed expansion ideas in QBR |
| 1/4 signals | Early | One signal only | Monitor — note signal, do not force the conversation |
| 0/4 signals | Not ready | None | Focus on value delivery and adoption first |

## Business Case Builder

When expansion signals are warm or hot, build a structured business case:

### 1. Current State
- What they have today (plan, seats, features)
- What they use vs what they are entitled to
- Value achieved with current investment

### 2. Expansion Opportunity
- What the expansion includes (additional seats, modules, tier upgrade)
- Why now (tie to the specific signals detected)
- What triggered this recommendation (data, not intuition)

### 3. Projected Value
- Expected outcomes from expansion (quantified where possible)
- Reference: similar customers who expanded and their results
- Time to value for the expansion

### 4. Investment Required
- Cost of expansion (if CSM has pricing authority)
- Or: "I will connect you with [AE/Sales] to discuss commercial terms"
- ROI framing: cost vs expected value

### 5. Recommended Next Step
- Specific CTA: demo of advanced features, pilot with new department, pricing call
- Timeline: when the decision should be made to align with their goals

## CS + Sales Partnership Model

### When CS Owns Expansion (Simple Upsell)
- More of the same: additional seats, higher usage tier, same product
- CSM has pricing authority for standard tier upgrades
- Customer relationship is the primary lever
- CSM executes end-to-end

### When CS Partners with Sales (Complex Expansion)
- New product line or module (cross-sell)
- New division or department (horizontal expansion)
- Large deal size (above CSM commercial authority)
- Competitive displacement in play
- CSM identifies and qualifies, Sales executes deal

### Handoff Protocol
CSM provides Sales with:
1. Account context brief (health score, relationship map, value delivered)
2. Expansion opportunity summary (signals, business case, timing)
3. Warm introduction to decision-maker
4. Agreement on who leads customer communication going forward

Sales provides CSM with:
1. Deal stage updates
2. Commercial terms for CSM awareness
3. Post-expansion success criteria for onboarding the new scope

## Conversation Guides

### Seeding Expansion (Light Touch)
"I noticed your team has been using [feature X] heavily — that is great to see. Have you considered [advanced capability]? Some of our customers in similar situations have found it saves an additional [metric]."

### Usage-Based Expansion (Direct)
"You are currently using [X%] of your [seats/credits/capacity]. Based on your growth trajectory, you will likely exceed your plan in the next [timeframe]. I want to make sure we get ahead of that so there is no disruption."

### Value-Based Expansion (Strategic)
"Given the [ROI/outcomes] we have achieved together, I think there is an opportunity to extend that value to [new use case/department]. Would it be helpful if I put together a business case for your team?"

### Timing-Based Expansion (Calendar-Aligned)
"I know your team starts budget planning in [month]. If expanding [product/seats/features] is something worth considering, now would be the time to build the case so it can be included in the planning cycle."
