# Brand narrative, content pillars and the 4 to 6 post series

Everything here is built from the person's own moments (positioning-and-bios.md, section 2). Where a moment is missing, the plan says what is needed; it never fills the gap. Where the facts or wording the person gave already support an angle or a post (a method they described, a public company line they quoted), write that angle or the post's working title and opening line as near-final copy built only from those facts, labelled for the person to confirm; placeholders stay only where a fact was not given. A pillar angle or post that no given fact covers carries a [Draft for you to confirm: ...] topic drawn from the pillar and the company's opened pages, with its source, that makes no claim about the person, whenever those pages support one; the moment it needs stays [User to add: ...] inside it. A bare placeholder stands in for a whole angle or a whole opening line only when neither the person's facts nor an opened company page supports a topic, or when the post's job is the person's own story (origin, lessons, what comes next).

## 1. Brand narrative (one page, first person, prose)

Five beats, each one or two short paragraphs:
1. Before: where the person started, told through one specific moment.
2. Turning point: what they saw, tried or got wrong that changed their view.
3. What they believe now: a stance a peer might disagree with, backed by what they lived. Not a slogan.
4. What they do now: for whom, and how, in the buyer's words.
5. What comes next: only what they can say in public today.

Checks: every beat traces to something the person said; no invented scene, feeling or dialogue; the employer's confidential matters stay out; lived experience leads and credentials stay in the background.

## 2. A single story (for a post, a talk opener or an About section)

Rewritten from a common story post shape:
- Scene: a time, a place and one specific detail the person gave.
- Stakes: what could have gone wrong.
- Problem: the obstacle or the mistake, told as it happened.
- What changed: what they did differently.
- Lesson: one insight a peer could use.
- Close: an open question that asks readers about their own experience.

Include one specific detail, one honest emotion and one usable insight, each from the person. Ask for any that is missing.

## 3. Content pillars (three to five)

Most of the person's posting and commenting should stay inside these pillars over months. For each pillar:

| Field | What goes in it |
|---|---|
| Pillar name | in the audience's words, not a coined label |
| Lived source | the roles, projects or moments that give the person a right to speak on it |
| Reader question | the question this pillar answers for the buyer or peer |
| Three post angles | each tied to a moment or a piece of work the person gave, or, where none is given, a [Draft for you to confirm: angle ...] from the pillar and the company's opened pages with its source, and the moment it needs as [User to add: ...] |
| Proof available | facts from the fact sheet the pillar can use |
| Out of bounds | what stays private (employer numbers, client names, roadmap) |
| Sector words | the vocabulary of the vertical (see verticals.md) |

## 4. The 4 to 6 post personal branding series

Rewritten from a personal branding series template. Ask for the number of posts (four to six), the audience, the goal (awareness, authority or opportunities, in the person's own words) and the cadence they can sustain.

| Post | Job | Built from |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Origin | how the person came to this work; the turning point | a moment from the narrative |
| 2. Method | how they actually work, in plain steps they used | a real project; no new coined framework |
| 3. Lessons | a mistake and what they would do differently | a moment the person is willing to share |
| 4. Contrast | a common belief they disagree with, and why, from experience | a stance from the narrative |
| 5. What's next (optional) | where their field is heading and their part in it | only public plans |
| 6. Practice (optional) | one thing readers can try, or a recap of parts 1 to 5 | the person's own checklist or decision steps |

Rules for every post in the series:
- Each post stands alone and also says where it sits ("Part 2 of 5").
- One idea per post.
- Opens with the person and what they did, or with "you" and the reader's situation; never with news, a trend or another company.
- A contrast early in the post (old way and new way, same task different result).
- Closes with a direct, open question.
- No links in the post body; links go in a comment.
- Two or three hashtags, one broad and the rest specific, kept consistent across the series.
- Visuals as separate images with real ALT text, not a PDF carousel.
- Facts needed: listed per post as [User to add: ...] until supplied.
- Opening lines: for a method, contrast, practice or reader question post that no given fact covers, the working title and the opening line are drafted from the pillar and the company's opened pages as [Draft for you to confirm: ...] with their source, with the person's own step, stance or answer as [User to add: ...] inside them (a post the given facts cover gets near-final lines from them, as part 2 of SKILL.md Example 4 does); only an origin, lessons or what's next post keeps the person's moment as the whole opening placeholder (as in SKILL.md Example 4).

## 5. Hand off for post drafting

If a skill for writing LinkedIn posts is installed in the session, pass it the fact sheet, the pillar, the post brief and the voice rules, and let it draft. If none is installed, draft here, run the short voice check (voice-check-short.md), count by script, and show the person the draft for their own edit. When the person edits it, review the edit; do not rewrite it.
