# GUARD Renewal Playbook — Defend Mode Reference

## 180-Day Countdown Cadence

### Day 180: Baseline Health Assessment
- Run Gauge mode to establish current health score
- Document value delivered since last renewal
- Identify any unresolved issues or feature gaps
- Map current stakeholder landscape (who is engaged, who left, who is new)
- Output: renewal risk score (Low/Medium/High/Critical)

### Day 120: Stakeholder Alignment Check
- Confirm executive sponsor is still engaged and supportive
- If champion changed: initiate relationship with replacement within 2 weeks
- Begin building the business case for renewal (value documentation)
- Confirm budget cycle timing — does this renewal align with their fiscal year?
- If multi-year: begin early negotiation exploration

### Day 90: Formal Renewal Conversation
- Present value recap (use Review mode QBR structure, Sections 1-2)
- Discuss forward plan and next-year objectives
- Surface any concerns proactively — do not wait for them to raise issues
- Confirm commercial terms are acceptable
- If at-risk: activate save strategy (see Save Playbook below)

### Day 60: Negotiation / Save Phase
- If healthy: this should be contract finalization and admin
- If at-risk: execute save strategy with urgency
- Involve CS leadership or VP Sales if escalation needed
- Address all outstanding issues before asking for signature

### Day 30: Final Commitment
- Contract should be signed or in final review by legal/procurement
- If unsigned: daily check-in, remove all remaining friction
- Prepare post-renewal success plan refresh

### Post-Renewal: Success Plan Reset
- Update success criteria for next contract period
- Reset health score baseline
- Plan first QBR of new contract period

## Renewal Probability Scoring

| Score Range | Probability | Label | Action |
|-------------|-------------|-------|--------|
| 80-100 | Very Likely | Green | Standard renewal process |
| 60-79 | Likely | Yellow | Proactive value reinforcement, address soft concerns |
| 40-59 | Uncertain | Orange | Formal save plan required, CS leadership involved |
| 20-39 | At Risk | Red | Executive intervention, competitive save strategy |
| 0-19 | Likely Lost | Black | Document lessons, prepare for churn, attempt Hail Mary |

### Probability Input Factors

Each factor contributes to the overall renewal probability:

| Factor | High Renewal Signal | Low Renewal Signal |
|--------|--------------------|--------------------|
| Product usage trend | Growing or stable | Declining |
| Exec sponsor engagement | Active, responsive | Disengaged or departed |
| Open escalations | None or resolved | Multiple unresolved |
| NPS trajectory | Improving or high stable | Declining |
| Competitor activity | None detected | Active POC or RFP |
| Payment behavior | On time | Delayed or disputing |
| Value documentation | Clear, measurable ROI | Cannot articulate value |
| Contract utilization | 70%+ of entitlement | Under 50% of entitlement |
| Champion status | Strong, multi-threaded | Single contact or no champion |
| Strategic alignment | Product on customer roadmap | Being evaluated for replacement |

## Save Strategy Playbook

### Save Strategy by Risk Type

**Champion Departure Save**
- Immediately identify the new decision-maker
- Request introduction from departing champion (if possible)
- Prepare a "new stakeholder brief" — what has been achieved, what is planned
- Offer a "success reset" meeting: re-establish goals with the new stakeholder
- Timeline: new relationship must be established within 30 days of departure

**Competitive Displacement Save**
- Identify which competitor and why
- Build a competitive comparison focused on switching costs and risk
- Offer a "stay incentive" if commercially viable (extended terms, additional value)
- Bring in exec sponsor or founder for a strategic conversation
- Never badmouth the competitor — focus on your unique value

**Value Perception Save**
- Run a value audit: document every outcome achieved (they may have forgotten)
- Present ROI analysis with their own data
- Offer a "value acceleration plan" with specific outcomes for next 90 days
- Address the perception gap: is it communication failure or actual product failure?

**Budget Constraint Save**
- Explore right-sizing: can they move to a lower tier vs cancelling entirely?
- Offer creative commercial terms: quarterly payment, deferred start
- Build a CFO-ready business case showing ROI exceeding cost
- Connect with budget decision-maker directly (not just the user)

**Product Gap Save**
- Confirm the gap is real and specific (not general dissatisfaction)
- Check roadmap: is the feature coming? If so, share timeline with commitment
- Offer a workaround: integration, manual process, professional services
- If the gap is fundamental and not on roadmap: be honest about it

## Conversation Guides

### Renewal Opener (Healthy Account)
"We are [X] months from renewal and I wanted to start the conversation early so there are no surprises on either side. Before we talk about next year, I want to make sure we have captured the value we have delivered together so far..."

### Renewal Opener (At-Risk Account)
"I know there have been some challenges over the past quarter and I want to address those directly. Before we discuss renewal, I want to understand where things stand from your perspective and what we need to fix..."

### Asking About Competitive Evaluation
"Are you looking at other options? I ask because I want to make sure we are giving you the best comparison to work with — and if there are gaps, I'd rather address them now than find out after a decision has been made."

### Handling "We Need to Think About It"
"Completely understand. Can I ask what specifically you are weighing? I may be able to provide information that helps the decision. And is there a timeline I should be aware of?"
